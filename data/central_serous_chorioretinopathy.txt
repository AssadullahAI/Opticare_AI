Central serous chorioretinopathy is a retinal condition where fluid accumulates under the retina.
It commonly affects young to middle-aged adults.
Patients may notice blurred or distorted central vision.
Straight lines may appear bent or wavy.
Objects may appear smaller or dimmer than normal.
Stress and corticosteroid use are known risk factors.
The condition often resolves on its own over time.
Chronic cases may require laser or medical treatment.
Optical coherence tomography is used for diagnosis.
Permanent vision loss is uncommon but possible in recurrent cases.
